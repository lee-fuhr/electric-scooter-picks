How to choose an electric scooter

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

Focus on your actual commute distance, terrain, and whether you’ll carry the scooter daily. Most advertised ranges shrink to about two-thirds in real-world use, so add 50% to your needs. Tires and motor wattage matter more for comfort and hill climbing than battery size alone.

How to estimate real-world range from manufacturer claims

Manufacturer range numbers are measured on flat, smooth pavement with a light rider at low speed. For the average adult on mixed terrain, expect roughly 65 percent of that claim. Hills, cold weather, and stop-and-go traffic cut it further. A scooter advertised at 20 miles might realistically cover 10 to 13 miles. To avoid getting stranded, measure your one-way commute distance, double it (round trip), then add 50 percent for real-world factors. If that number exceeds the adjusted range of a scooter, look for a model with a larger battery or consider charging at your destination.

Motor wattage for flat vs hilly terrain

Motor wattage is about torque, not outright speed. A 250W motor handles flat pavement and gentle slopes fine, but on hills steeper than 5 percent grade it will slow dramatically. A 350W motor is the minimum for moderate hills, and 500W or more keeps you climbing at a reasonable pace without draining the battery. Pay attention to continuous (nominal) wattage rather than peak wattage, which is a short burst and less meaningful. If your commute includes any sustained uphill, stepping up one power tier makes a much bigger difference than adding battery capacity.

Why tire type is more important than most buyers realize

Pneumatic (air-filled) tires offer the best ride comfort and grip, especially on rough pavement. Their main downside is punctures, though self-healing tubes or tire sealant greatly reduce that risk. Solid tires never go flat, but they transmit every bump and have noticeably less traction on wet roads. If your daily route has potholes, brickwork, or loose debris, pneumatic tires are worth the maintenance. If you ride only on smooth, dry paths, solid tires can be fine. Hybrid options like airless honeycomb tires exist but are still less common in budget models.

Weight: the most underrated spec

A scooter that weighs 30 pounds feels manageable to carry up a flight of stairs. At 40 or 45 pounds, that same task becomes a daily chore you’ll resent. Weight affects everything: how often you take it on public transit, whether you’ll carry it into a store, and how quickly the folding latch wears. Folding mechanism quality is paired with weight, a heavy scooter with a flimsy latch will loosen over thousands of cycles. Look for metal latches with a positive click, and read owner feedback about durability after a few months of use.

IP ratings, folding mechanisms, and riding in the rain

An IP54 rating means the scooter resists dust and splashes, but it is not waterproof. For regular rain riding, look for at least IPX5 (water jets) and avoid submerging the deck. Wet brakes and reduced traction are hazards regardless of rating, treat rain riding as a compromise. The folding latch is the mechanical part that fails most often. A well-built latch locks firmly with no play. Budget models may use plastic components that wear quickly. If you fold the scooter several times daily, invest in a model with a proven, robust latch.

Weight limits and how riding near the max changes everything

Riding near the scooter’s weight limit strains the motor, drains the battery faster, and increases stopping distance. Even at 80 percent of the limit, real-world range can drop by 20 to 30 percent compared to a lighter rider. Climbing hills at high load also accelerates motor wear. If you are close to the published weight limit, choose a scooter rated 20 to 30 pounds above your weight. That buffer gives better range, hill performance, and longevity. Ignoring this spec often leads to premature battery and motor issues.

Frequently asked questions

What three questions should I ask myself before buying an electric scooter?

First, what is my actual one-way commute distance? Add 50 percent for real-world conditions. Second, will I carry it indoors or up stairs regularly? If yes, weight and folding design matter more than top speed or range. Third, am I on hilly terrain? If yes, prioritize motor wattage (at least 350W) over battery size.

When does spending more on a premium scooter actually pay off?

A premium scooter is worth the extra cost when you need suspension, larger pneumatic tires with sealant, a robust folding latch that lasts years, and a water-resistance rating that handles daily rain. For flat, short commutes on smooth pavement, a well-reviewed budget model is often the smarter, more affordable choice.

Can I ride an electric scooter in the rain?

Only if the scooter has an IP rating of at least IPX5, which resists water jets. Even then, avoid puddles deeper than the deck height. Wet surfaces reduce traction and brake performance, so ride cautiously. An IP54 rating is splash-resistant but not recommended for regular rain exposure.

How much maintenance do electric scooters require?

Typical maintenance includes checking tire pressure weekly for pneumatic tires, lubricating the folding latch every few months, and replacing brake pads after roughly 300 to 500 miles. Battery health declines over time; expect noticeable range reduction after two to three years of regular use.

Does a higher-wattage motor always mean a faster scooter?

Not necessarily. Wattage primarily affects torque and hill-climbing ability, not top speed, many 250W and 500W scooters have the same legal speed cap. Higher wattage gives you better acceleration and the ability to maintain speed on uphills, but it won’t make you faster on flat ground.

In shortThe right electric scooter matches your specific commute distance, terrain, and how often you’ll carry it. Ignoring factors like real-world range, tire type, and your own weight will lead to disappointment. A budget scooter on flat, smooth roads can be a fantastic buy, but if you face hills, rain, or regular carrying, spending a bit more on a premium model with proven build quality will save you frustration down the road.
